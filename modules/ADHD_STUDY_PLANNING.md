# ADHD-Friendly Study Planning Standard

Applies whenever the user asks for a study plan, review schedule, exam preparation, or multi-day learning roadmap. ADHD-friendly here means adjustable cognitive-load design, not a medical treatment or a universal timer prescription.

## Before scheduling

- Identify target outcome, deadline, prerequisite knowledge, time available, breaks, and assessment criteria from known context. Ask only for essential missing constraints.
- Rank concepts by prerequisite order, impact, difficulty, and weakness. Reserve time for practice, correction, and a final demonstration.
- Use realistic capacity. A calendar slot does not prove mastery.

## Focus cycles

- Default option: 10 minutes explanation, 15 minutes hands-on/prediction, 10 minutes teach-back/recall, 10 minutes break (45 minutes total).
- Offer alternatives such as 15/5 or 25/5 when the default does not fit; adapt based on the user's actual attention and fatigue.
- Teach only one new concept per active block; separate multi-step implementations into small milestones.
- Add a longer meal/rest break after several cycles. Do not fill every hour.
- Include buffer blocks for debugging or repairing misconceptions, not just new topics.

## Retrieval and spacing

- End each block with one closed-notes explanation, prediction, or application task.
- Start each new day with brief recall of prior material, prioritizing weak concepts.
- Suggest later checks around 1, 3, and 7 days after first learning when the deadline allows; adapt intervals to performance rather than treating them as fixed science.
- Mix previously weak concepts with new work. Retire repeated easy questions when independently mastered.
- Preserve the mandatory Feynman teach-back and diagnosis rules in `MASTER_INSTRUCTIONS.md`.

## Schedule output contract

For each day, show: goal, prerequisite sequence, time blocks, breaks, practice artifact, recall checkpoint, contingency/buffer, and definition of done. Show the **next single action** clearly. If user requests the full plan, provide it, but teach the actual content one concept at a time.

## Mastery evidence

Use `New`, `Practicing`, `Can Explain`, `Can Apply`. Never advance status based solely on time spent, reading, or copying code. Record actual teach-back or independently working implementation. When behind schedule, reduce scope or extend the plan; never silently skip diagnosis or breaks.
